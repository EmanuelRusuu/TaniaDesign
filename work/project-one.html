Type

Overview

Briefly set the scene: what is this product, who is it for, and what was the opportunity? Keep it to a few sentences — you'll go deeper below.

The challenge

Describe the problem you were solving. What made the existing experience frustrating, confusing, or slow? A clear problem statement here shows you design with intent, not just aesthetics.

Design question: How might we help [users] [do the thing] more easily and with more confidence?

Research & discovery

Summarize how you explored the problem — interviews, a survey, competitor teardowns, or reviewing existing solutions. Pull out 2–3 insights that shaped your decisions.

  • Insight 1 — what you learned and why it mattered.
  • Insight 2 — a surprising finding that changed direction.
  • Insight 3 — a need the current options weren't meeting.

Wireframes & flow

Show how you moved from problem to structure — user flows and low-fidelity wireframes. This is where you prove you thought about hierarchy and usability before visuals.

Final UI

Present the high-fidelity screens. Talk through the visual choices — type, color, spacing — and any reusable components or a mini design system you built.

Outcome & reflection

Wrap up: what did the final design achieve, what did you learn, and what would you improve with more time? Honest reflection reads as maturity — it's exactly what a junior candidate should show.
